I am a doctoral student at the University of Idaho working with Dr. Scott Grieshaber. My research focuses on how Chlamydia trachomatis moves between infectious and replicative stages during its developmental cycle. I use molecular and cell biology approaches to study the regulation and timing of these transitions.

01 / INTRODUCTION

About me

My research sits at the intersection of microbiology, molecular genetics, and cellular development.

I’m currently pursuing my Ph.D. in Biological Sciences, concentrating in Microbiology, Molecular Biology and Biochemistry, at the University of Idaho with Dr. Scott Grieshaber. One question that keeps me curious is how a bacterium knows when to switch from an infectious form to a form that grows and replicates. That question is at the heart of my research on Chlamydia trachomatis.

My path here hasn’t been a straight line, and I value that. From studying antibiotic-resistant bacteria in Bangladesh to learning molecular cloning and working with rainbow trout at Washington State University, each experience has given me a new way to think about science. I enjoy the hands-on work of experiments as much as the conversations and collaborations that help turn a result into a better question.

02 / RESEARCH

Research

Current doctoral research and previous projects in molecular biology, microbiology, and biotechnology.

CURRENT RESEARCH · 2026 to PRESENT

Understanding the developmental cycle of Chlamydia trachomatis

C. trachomatis alternates between the infectious elementary body (EB) and replicative reticulate body (RB). My doctoral research examines how these transitions are timed and regulated, including variability between individual bacteria. Using stage-specific markers and gene-expression analyses, I am investigating developmental regulators such as Euo, HctA, and HctB to better understand bacterial differentiation and the formation of infectious progeny.

VOLUNTEER RESEARCHER · 2025 to 2026

Molecular cloning and transgenic rainbow trout

At Washington State University’s Aquatic Molecular Physiology Lab, I assisted with Tol2 plasmid constructs for stable transgenesis in rainbow trout and carried out fluorescence screening of embryos to assess transgene expression. This work strengthened my experience with molecular protocols, microscopy, and model-organism care.

Washington State University
UNDERGRADUATE THESIS · 2023 to 2024

Green silver nanoparticles for antimicrobial applications

For my undergraduate thesis, I synthesized silver nanoparticles using Codiaeum variegatum leaf extract, characterized them using UV, visible spectrophotometry, and evaluated antibacterial activity against multidrug-resistant bacteria isolated from respiratory disease samples.

Islamic University, Bangladesh

Additional previous work includes isolation of salt-tolerant bacteria from Sundarbans soil, plant-based nanoparticle synthesis, and computational molecular docking.
